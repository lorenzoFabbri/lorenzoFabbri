I'm **Lorenzo Fabbri**, a cancer epidemiologist at the Spanish National Cancer Research Centre (CNIO) in Madrid, where I am a postdoctoral fellow in the Genetic and Molecular Epidemiology Group and hold a Juan de la Cierva fellowship (2026–2029). My research asks what medicines do to the risk of cancer. I estimate the effect of starting one drug rather than another by emulating target trials on routine health records, mainly in pancreatic and bladder cancer, and report each result across social groups where the data allow.

My training is in environmental epidemiology and causal inference. For my PhD in Biomedicine at Universitat Pompeu Fabra (2021–2025), in the Childhood and Environment group at ISGlobal under Prof. Martine Vrijheid and Dr. Léa Maitre, I studied early-life exposure to environmental chemicals and child neurodevelopment in the HELIX and ATHLETE cohorts, using the parametric g-formula, evidence triangulation and multi-omic data. Between the PhD and CNIO I was a postdoctoral researcher in the Rakislova Lab at ISGlobal, working on the cancer genomics of vulvar and penile squamous cell carcinoma.

I am also studying for further degrees in public health (UNED), social inequalities (UNED), theoretical statistics (the Open University) and economics (UOC). I write open-source software for causal inference and reproducible research: I lead [etverse](https://etverse.github.io/), an R ecosystem for transparent causal inference, maintain [forrest](https://cran.r-project.org/package=forrest) and [orcidtr](https://cran.r-project.org/package=orcidtr) on CRAN, and wrote [bibaudit](https://pypi.org/project/bibaudit/), a Python package that checks a bibliography field by field against Crossref, DataCite and PubMed.
